# The 2026 Legislative Blackout Period

THE 2026 LEGISLATIVE
BLACKOUT PERIOD

Ala. Code § 17-5-7 (b)(2)
“[C}andidates for legislative and statewide office and their principal
campaign committees may not accept, solicit, or receive contributions
during the period when the Legislature is convened in session. For
purposes of this section, the Legislature is convened in session at any time
from the opening day of the special or regular session and continued
through the day of adjournment sine die for that session. However, this
subdivision shall not apply within 120 days of any primary, runoff, or
general election, and shall not apply to the candidates or their
principal campaign committees participating in any special election as
called by the Governor. This subdivision shall not apply to a loan from a
candidate to his or her own principal campaign committee.”

LEGISLATIVE BLACKOUT DETAILS
• What is the legislative blackout?
• Prohibits candidates for legislative and statewide office from accepting,
soliciting, or receiving contributions while the legislature is in session.

• When is the legislative blackout?
• First Day of 2026 Legislative Session and the legislative blackout is
January 13, 2026 beginning at 12:01am. See AGO No. 94-00228.
• Last day of the 2026 legislative blackout is January 19, 2026. The
blackout extends to 12:00am the morning of January 20th.

• Who does the legislative blackout apply to?
• The legislative blackout only applies to candidates for legislative and
statewide office; it does not apply to candidates for any other office.
• The legislative blackout does not apply to sitting Legislators running for
county or federal office. AGO Nos. 2000-088 and 2002-040.

CONTRIBUTIONS DURING THE BLACKOUT PERIOD
• Contributions received prior to the blackout but reported on an
FCPA report during the blackout are not affected by the blackout.
• Contributions received during the blackout must be returned to
the donor.
• It is recommended that candidates deactivate any webpages that
accept contributions online to avoid inadvertently accepting funds
during the blackout.

CHECKS
• The Code of Alabama § 17-5-2(a)(3)(c) provides, “the date of receipt of a
contribution shall be the first date the recipient of the contribution is able
to make use of the contribution. In the case of a check, the date of receipt is
the earlier of the following:
1. Ten days from the date that the check came within the recipient’s control.
2. The date that the check was deposited into the recipient’s account.”

EXCEPTIONS TO THE LEGISLATIVE BLACKOUT
• Within 120 days of an election.
• Loans from a candidate to his or her own Principal Campaign
Committee.
• Does not affect Political Action Committees raising money. However,
PACs may not contribute money to legislative or statewide candidates
during blackout.
• Does not affect candidates in special elections.

EXAMPLE SCENARIOS
• I am a candidate for a legislative or statewide office, but I do not currently hold office.
Am I affected by the legislative blackout? Yes.
• I am a candidate for a legislative or statewide office, and I want to spend money that
my principal campaign committee already has in the campaign account. May I do this
during the blackout? Yes.
• I have yet to qualify with my political party to run for a legislative or statewide office
and have yet to raise any money. Does the blackout apply to me? If you are a candidate
for a legislative or statewide office, the blackout affects you no matter of
qualification status. If you are a candidate for any other office, the blackout doesn’t
apply to you at all.
• I have a PayPal, GoFundMe, or similar webpage that accepts contributions. I received
electronic contributions from the webpage during the blackout, but they were given by
the donor prior to the blackout. Can I keep the contribution? No. You received the
contribution into your custody during the blackout.

EXAMPLE SCENARIOS
I am a candidate for a legislative or statewide office, and I received a contribution the
day(s) prior to the legislative blackout. The person that handles my bank account did
not deposit the money into my account until the blackout already started. Must I return
the contribution? The Code of Alabama § 17-5-2 provides that “[i]n the case of a
check, the date of receipt is the earlier of the following:
1. Ten days from the date that the check came within the recipient’s control.
2. The date that the check was deposited into the recipient’s account.”
• If you received a contribution (other than a check) during the legislative blackout,
it is an impermissible contribution and you must return it.
