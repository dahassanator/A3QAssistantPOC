# The Alabama Ethics Commission's Role Under the FCPA

This file is a summary assembled from other documents in this knowledge base, not a single source document. It exists because staff often assume the Secretary of State handles everything about campaign finance, and that is not correct.

## Who does what

The Secretary of State runs the filing system, publishes the calendars and guidelines, receives the reports, and levies civil penalties for reports that are filed late. See §§ 17-5-9, 17-5-11 and 17-5-19.1(a).

The Ethics Commission has interpretive authority over the Fair Campaign Practices Act. The Secretary of State's own PAC Filing Guidelines 2026 state this directly: "The Alabama Ethics Commission has interpretive authority over the Fair Campaign Practices Act. PAC's interested in an official opinion may seek one with the Ethics Commission." So a committee that wants an official ruling on what the FCPA requires asks the Ethics Commission, not the Secretary of State.

## The three places the Ethics Commission appears in the statute

1. Inaccurate reports. Under § 17-5-19.1(a), the Secretary of State penalizes a failure to file on time, but the State Ethics Commission is the body that may levy an administrative penalty against a person who files a materially inaccurate report and does not correct it.

2. Appeals of civil penalties. Under § 17-5-19.2, a person penalized under § 17-5-19.1 files a written notice with the Secretary of State within 14 days, and the Secretary of State refers the review to the State Ethics Commission. The Commission may set aside or reduce the penalty on a showing of good cause, and the person seeking review bears the burden of proof.

3. Definitions. Under § 17-5-2(a)(2), "Commission" in the FCPA means the State Ethics Commission created under § 36-25-3, and under § 17-5-2(b) the FCPA's terms carry the meanings given in the Ethics Law at § 36-25-1.

## The ethics law is a separate body of law

The FCPA governs campaign money. The Ethics Law, Ala. Code § 36-25-1 and following, governs conflicts of interest, gifts, and the Statement of Economic Interests. They are different statutes with different filings, and a candidate is subject to both. AG Opinion 2000-088 makes the separation explicit: after answering an FCPA question it adds, "This opinion addresses the applicability of the Fair Campaign Practices Act. You may wish to seek an opinion from the State Ethics Commission concerning the applicability of the ethics law."
