# Alabama AG Opinion 94-00228

Office of the Attorney General, State of Alabama. Jimmy Evans, Attorney General. Dated July 11, 1994.

To: Honorable Jim Bennett, Secretary of State, P.O. Box 5616, Montgomery, AL 36103

Subject headnote: Fair Campaign Practices Act - Candidates - Campaign Contributions - Reporting Requirements - Contributions

Headnote holdings:

- The opening day of the legislative session begins at 12:01 a.m. on the day the Legislature convenes.
- A campaign contribution that is unwanted or unsolicited and not otherwise utilized to influence an election may be returned to the contributor and not considered a contribution that is accepted and reportable under the Fair Campaign Practices Act.

Dear Mr. Bennett:

This opinion is issued in response to your request for an opinion from the Attorney General.

## Question 1

Is opening day for Section 17-22A-7(b) of the FCPA defined as the calendar day beginning at 12:01 a.m. on the day the Legislature convenes or is the opening day defined as the legislative day that begins with the fall of the gavel?

### Facts and Analysis

Code of Alabama 1975, § 17-22A-7(b), provides:

> "Candidates for state offices and their principal campaign committees . . . may not solicit or accept, or both, contributions during the period when the legislature is convened in session. For the purposes of this section, the legislature is considered convened in session at any time from the opening day of the special or regular session and continuing through the day of adjournment sine die for that session. . . ." (Emphasis added.)

Opening day is not defined by the Code, therefore, it must be given its common, everyday meaning. A day begins at 12:01 a.m. Thus, the opening day begins at 12:01 a.m. on the day the Legislature convenes.

The language used in the above provision is somewhat confusing and we understand that there was some confusion by various candidates concerning this issue. Accordingly, it is our opinion that this opinion should be applied prospectively.

### Conclusion

Given the foregoing, the opening day of the legislative session begins at 12:01 a.m. on the day the Legislature convenes. Given the confusion surrounding this issue, this opinion should be applied prospectively.

## Question 2

If a candidate or elected official receives an unsolicited and unwanted campaign contribution and returns it without ever depositing it to the principal campaign committee's account, must the contribution be listed on the committee's itemized contribution report?

### Facts and Analysis

Contributions for purposes of the Fair Campaign Practices Act are defined in Code of Alabama 1975, § 17-22A-2(2), and includes the following:

> "1. A gift, subscription, loan, advance, deposit of money or anything of value, a payment, a forgiveness of a loan, or payment of a third party, made for the purpose of influencing the results of an election;"

Neither this provision nor any other provision of the Fair Campaign Practices Act specifically address unsolicited or returned contributions. It is our opinion, however, that a campaign contribution that is unwanted or unsolicited and not otherwise utilized to influence an election may be returned to the contributor and should not be treated as a contribution that is accepted and reportable under the Fair Campaign Practices Act. If a candidate wishes to do so, he may voluntarily report the receipt of the contribution on the itemized contribution report and report the return of the contribution on the itemized expenditure report to reflect that the contribution was received and returned. However, if a contribution is deposited in any campaign account or is otherwise utilized to influence an election, it must be reported as a contribution. The contribution may be returned and this should be reported on the itemized expenditure report.

### Conclusion

A campaign contribution that is unwanted or unsolicited and not otherwise utilized to influence an election may be returned to the contributor and not considered a contribution that is accepted and reportable under the Fair Campaign Practices Act.
